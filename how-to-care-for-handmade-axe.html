How to Care for a Handmade Axe: Cleaning, Rust Prevention & Proper Storage

A practical guide to maintaining steel, protecting wooden handles and preserving the craftsmanship of your handmade axe.

A handmade axe is more than a simple cutting tool. It represents traditional craftsmanship, careful material selection and the skill involved in shaping steel and wood into a functional piece. Whether you own a traditional wood axe, a forged steel axe or a Viking-inspired design, regular maintenance helps preserve its appearance and condition.

Knowing how to care for a handmade axe does not require an elaborate workshop or expensive equipment. A few sensible habits can make a significant difference: cleaning the head after use, keeping moisture away, checking the handle and storing the axe properly.

This guide explains practical axe maintenance steps for collectors, outdoor enthusiasts and owners of handmade axes. It also covers common mistakes that can shorten the useful life of an axe.

1. Why Handmade Axe Maintenance Matters

Most traditional handmade axes combine a steel head with a wooden handle. Both materials need attention. Steel can develop rust when moisture remains on its surface, while wood can expand or shrink as humidity and temperature change.

Neglecting these materials can affect the axe's condition. Rust may damage the surface, an untreated handle may become rough or dry, and a loose head can create a serious safety concern.

Regular maintenance helps you:

  • Protect the steel head from moisture and corrosion.
  • Keep the wooden handle in good condition.
  • Identify damage before it becomes more serious.
  • Preserve the original appearance and craftsmanship.
  • Maintain the tool for responsible long-term ownership.

2. How to Clean a Handmade Axe After Use

Cleaning is one of the simplest and most important parts of axe care. Dirt, tree sap, moisture and other residue should not be left on the head for extended periods.

Remove dirt and sap

Use a clean, dry cloth to remove loose dirt and moisture. If tree sap or stubborn residue remains, use a suitable cleaner according to its instructions and ensure that no cleaner residue remains on the steel.

Dry the steel head completely

Pay attention to the cutting edge, sides of the head and areas around the eye where the handle passes through. Water left in small gaps can encourage corrosion. Never put a wet axe away for long-term storage.

Clean the handle carefully

Wipe the wooden handle with a dry or slightly damp cloth when needed. Avoid soaking the wood or leaving the axe exposed to standing water. Allow any remaining moisture to dry before storage.

The USDA Forest Service's Ax Maintenance Manual also discusses cleaning the head and protecting the cutting edge as part of routine care.

3. How to Prevent Rust on a Steel Axe

Steel contains iron, which can react with moisture and oxygen. This means even a well-made forged steel axe can develop rust if it is repeatedly exposed to damp conditions.

After cleaning and drying the head, apply a thin protective coating of an oil suitable for steel. Follow the product manufacturer's directions and wipe away excess oil. The goal is to create a light protective barrier rather than leave the axe greasy.

Practical rust prevention habits

  • Dry the axe head after every use, especially after working in wet conditions.
  • Apply a suitable protective oil before storing the axe for an extended period.
  • Keep the axe away from damp floors and humid storage areas.
  • Check the steel periodically for early signs of surface rust.
  • Make sure the blade cover is dry before placing it over the edge.

These steps are particularly useful for owners of carbon steel axes. A naturally finished or polished steel surface still needs sensible moisture protection.

For additional guidance, see Gränsfors Bruk's axe care recommendations, which explain why steel heads should be protected against moisture.

4. How to Maintain a Wooden Axe Handle

The handle is just as important as the steel head. Many handmade axes feature wooden handles because wood provides a traditional appearance and a practical grip. However, wood responds to changes in moisture and temperature.

Inspect the handle regularly for cracks, splinters, unusual movement or signs of damage. A handle that feels loose or structurally compromised should not be used until it has been properly inspected and repaired or replaced.

Use a suitable wood conditioner

Depending on the handle's finish and manufacturer's instructions, suitable wood treatments may include boiled linseed oil or a purpose-made wood conditioner. Apply the product sparingly and allow it to dry as directed.

Do not assume that every coating is suitable for every handle finish. Test and follow the product guidance where appropriate.

Check the head-to-handle fit

The axe head must remain securely fitted to the handle. Temperature and humidity changes can affect wood, so check the fit routinely. If the head moves, stop using the axe and have the problem corrected before further use.

Never attempt to solve a loose-head problem simply by continuing to use the tool. The manufacturer's guidance from Gränsfors Bruk's axe care guide also highlights the importance of maintaining the handle and checking its fit.

5. Proper Storage for Handmade Axes

Good storage protects both the steel head and the wooden handle. An axe should be kept in a clean, moderately dry environment where it is not exposed to rain, condensation or extreme temperature changes.

Follow these storage practices:

  • Clean and dry the axe before putting it away.
  • Apply a light protective coating to the steel when appropriate.
  • Use a suitable blade cover to protect the cutting edge.
  • Ensure the leather or other protective sheath is completely dry.
  • Store the axe securely where children and unauthorized users cannot access it.
  • Avoid leaving the axe in a damp shed, wet vehicle or exposed outdoor area.

Do not store a damp leather sheath against the steel. Trapped moisture can encourage rust. A dry, ventilated storage location is a better choice for long-term care.

6. Sharpening and Edge Maintenance

A cutting edge gradually becomes dull through use. Regular inspection helps you understand when maintenance is needed. The correct sharpening method depends on the axe design, edge profile and manufacturer's recommendations.

Use appropriate sharpening equipment and maintain the intended edge shape. A whetstone or suitable axe sharpening tool may be used for routine edge care. More substantial damage may require professional attention.

Sharpening safety

  • Secure the axe properly before working on its edge.
  • Wear suitable protective gloves and eye protection.
  • Keep your hands away from the cutting path.
  • Follow the sharpening tool manufacturer's instructions.
  • Do not use a damaged or unstable axe during sharpening.

Avoid unnecessary grinding that changes the original edge geometry. For detailed maintenance and sharpening information, consult the USDA Forest Service Ax Manual.

7. Common Axe Maintenance Mistakes to Avoid

Even owners who regularly clean their tools can make mistakes that affect long-term condition. Avoid these common problems:

Leaving the axe wet

Moisture left on steel can encourage rust. Always dry the head before storage.

Ignoring handle damage

Small cracks, splinters and loose fittings should not be overlooked. Stop using an axe if its handle or head is unsafe.

Using the axe for unintended tasks

An axe is designed for particular purposes. Do not use an ordinary axe as a hammer, pry bar or wedge unless the manufacturer specifically states that the model is designed for that use.

Storing it in an unsuitable environment

Excessive dampness and extreme heat can affect both steel and wood. Choose a secure and appropriate storage location.

Using the wrong maintenance products

Some cleaning chemicals, coatings and abrasive materials may damage finishes or leave harmful residue. Choose products suitable for the axe's materials and follow their instructions.

9. Frequently Asked Questions About Handmade Axe Care

How often should I oil a handmade axe?

There is no single schedule for every axe. It depends on storage conditions, use and exposure to moisture. Inspect the steel regularly and apply a suitable protective oil whenever needed, especially before extended storage.

Can a steel axe develop rust?

Yes. Steel can develop rust when exposed to moisture and oxygen. Cleaning, thorough drying and suitable protective oil help reduce this risk.

How should I care for a wooden axe handle?

Keep the handle clean and avoid soaking it. Inspect it for cracks or splinters and use a suitable wood conditioner according to the manufacturer's recommendations.

Can I store my axe with its leather sheath?

Yes, provided the sheath and axe head are completely dry. A damp sheath can trap moisture against the steel and encourage corrosion.

How do I know if my axe handle is loose?

Check for movement between the head and handle. If the head shifts, stop using the axe and arrange proper inspection and repair.

What is the best way to preserve a handmade axe?

Clean it after use, keep the steel dry, protect it against rust, maintain the wooden handle, inspect its condition and store it securely in a suitable environment.

10. Final Thoughts

A handmade axe deserves regular attention. Its steel head and wooden handle respond differently to moisture, temperature and everyday use, so both parts need appropriate care.

By cleaning the axe after use, protecting the steel from rust, checking the handle, maintaining the edge and choosing proper storage, you can help preserve its condition for years to come.
